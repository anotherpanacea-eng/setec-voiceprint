# SVP Surface Harness — a forward-only CLI/envelope/report harness, plus a structurally-verified path to adopting it in guard-protected modules

**Status:** Draft (proposal — independent adversarial review required before any build) · **Date:** 2026-08-06 · **Repo:** `setec-voiceprint`

**Provenance:** `specs/svp-packaging-conversion.md` §3 explicitly withdrew `setec.surfaces.harness` and all surface-boilerplate consolidation from its own scope: "Any future shared CLI/envelope/report harness is a separate specification and independent review after the relocation settles; it cannot enter as a packaging cleanup." This is that separate specification. It does not reopen packaging's P0–P5 scope and proposes no change to `plugins/setec-voiceprint/scripts/setec/`'s layout, the not-yet-built layering checker's rules, or `packaging_move_map.json`'s existing schema. It is independently sequenced (see §9) and, on the evidence below, does not need to wait for packaging's P4.

**This draft went through three revision rounds during authoring, all recorded here because the reasoning is load-bearing for review:** an initial pass scoped a guard amendment; a strategic re-adjudication cut it entirely to a forward-only ratchet, reasoning that `tools/check_claim_license_guard.py`'s lack of judgment is its strength and any amendment is attack surface; the owner then overruled that cut, on the grounds that a breaking `ClaimLicense` contract change (schema 2.0, a new required field, a changed `reason_category` taxonomy) is expected within roughly a year, will fan out to all guard-protected call sites regardless of whether this spec exists, and should get calm design and adversarial review now rather than a rushed amendment under deadline pressure later. That is the condition under which this document should be re-read if it sits unbuilt: if a breaking `ClaimLicense`-contract change becomes concretely scheduled, the guard amendment in §6 stops being optional infrastructure and becomes the difference between a reviewed mechanism and an ad hoc one. A subsequent adversarial review of the restored §6 (recorded against PR #390) then found the first drafted mechanism itself **exploitable** — the rule registry, as first written, was readable from the PR's own working tree, which collapses to the exact killed `old_symbol`/`new_symbol` laundering shape §6.2 rejects, just wearing a `.py` extension instead of `.json`. §6 below is the corrected mechanism; §6.11 walks the constructed attack against it, line by line, and states plainly that it no longer succeeds.

## 0. Executive summary

1. **Mandatory forward-only ratchet** (§5): every *new* top-level surface must use the harness's CLI/envelope/report/`_emit` helpers; a new checker blocks a new hand-rolled copy of that scaffolding. This is the durable win — 54 new `capabilities.d` entries landed in the 60 days before this spec was drafted, so the growth channel this closes is active, not hypothetical. It touches zero existing files and cannot conflict with the claim-license guard, because it only inspects paths absent from the merge base.
2. **Optional adoption of the 30 modules already outside the guard's protected closure** (§7): real, but small. Only 4 of those 30 (4,255 LOC) actually build a `output_schema.build_output`/`build_error_output` envelope at all — the other 26 are acquisition/utility scripts with no envelope to standardize. Net yield: on the order of 300–500 LOC. Worth doing because it is free (no guard interaction whatsoever), not because it is the point.
3. **A guard amendment authorizing adoption inside the protected closure** (§6): the hard part, and now on its second design after an adversarial review found the first one exploitable (§6.11). The corrected mechanism never compares against anything a PR can author, at any layer: the "before" side of the module diff is re-derived from the merge base by CI as it is today, **and, new in this revision, the rule registry itself is also re-derived from the merge base** — a rule introduced or edited in a PR has no effect on that same PR's verification, closing the loophole where PR-supplied Python was, in substance, the same author-controlled input the killed `old_symbol`/`new_symbol` JSON row was (§6.2, §6.11). It authorizes only a small, closed, *code*-defined registry of structural rewrites whose insertions are mechanically forbidden from carrying any string content (§6.3), each proven per adoption against an **intra-module dataflow closure** — not a name-pattern scan — that traces every value reachable from a `ClaimLicense(...)` call or `_claim_license*` function, including list-mutation call sites like `caveats.append(...)`, to its binding sites anywhere in the module (§6.4).
4. **Sizing, sampled directly, not inherited, and widened after adversarial spot-checks:** six real surfaces — four read in full during drafting, two more identified by adversarial review and structurally corroborated here — show a **wide, long-tailed** range: 0.8%–11.1% of file LOC (mean 6.3%, but the mean is not a reliable population estimate given the spread — see §3.4) is mechanically extractable once claim-license content and domain/posture logic are correctly excluded. The honest population estimate is **roughly 3,000–10,000 LOC, heavily concentrated in a minority of harness-shaped families (the judge-based-scan family samples at 10.9%–11.1%), with a long tail of files near zero** (files that inline argparse in `main()` with no separate `build_arg_parser`/`_emit` at all score 0.8%–5.3%). This is stated as a range with a known shape, not as a figure "consistent with" any prior estimate — the original ~31k audit figure and the ~5–8k LOC estimate this spec was initially briefed against are both function-name-pattern artifacts, and treating a small, non-random sample as validating either would launder sample bias into a population claim. Increment 1's actual, rule-registry-limited yield is far smaller still: on the order of a few hundred LOC (§8).
5. **Sequencing:** adopt in place on the flat tree, not gated on packaging's P4 relocation (§9), and demonstrated invariant under P2 specifically, which is queued now (§9.1) — every rule in §6.3 matches statements inside surface modules, none of which P2's compatibility-launcher contract touches.
6. **Weakest point, stated plainly, not buried:** the guard amendment is more code than the guard has ever run before, and this revision adds more still (registry merge-base sourcing, a no-string-content self-check, an intra-module dataflow closure, a registration-staleness shortcut, rule-ordering/overlap checks). More code executing inside a fail-closed gate is more surface for a subtle bug that manufactures a false pass. §6.8 names the mitigation (a golden-fixture suite, now including per-operation-kind adversarial fixtures and a mutation-testing requirement, as a merge-blocking part of the amendment itself) and states directly why that mitigation is necessary rather than sufficient.

### 1.1 Existing regression coverage this spec must not contradict

`plugins/setec-voiceprint/scripts/tests/test_check_claim_license_guard.py` already pins the exact failure mode this spec's amendment must avoid reintroducing. `test_moves_substring_laundering_is_blocked`'s docstring records that an earlier revision of the guard's move-map handling did a raw `str.replace` on the whole `ast.dump()` **text** keyed by a committed `old_symbol`/`new_symbol` pair, and that `old_symbol="REPORTS"` / `new_symbol="REFUSES"` could silently invert a license sentence containing "REPORTS" as a substring while being "explained" by a bogus symbol-rename row. The fix — visible in the current `tools/check_claim_license_guard.py`, which no longer contains any `_normalized_dump` or text-substitution code path at all — was to make `load_move_map` reject any non-empty `moves`/`path_rewrites` outright at P1, deferring the entire question of *how* a legitimate structural delta gets authorized to whichever phase first needs one. That deferred question is what §6 answers. §6.2 lists the shapes rejected for the same reason this test pins, §6.7 restates why the corrected mechanism cannot repeat the failure, and §6.11 walks the specific same-PR variant of it that adversarial review constructed against this document's own first draft.

## 2. Scope

**The harness owns**, for any surface that adopts it:

- Common CLI plumbing: `argparse.ArgumentParser` construction (`prog`/`description` passthrough) and exactly three shared flags — `--json`, `--out`, `--out-md`. Nothing else. Every domain-specific flag (`--judge`, `--cap-per-question`, `--function-top`, …) stays authored in the surface's own `build_arg_parser`.
- Envelope composition **shape**, via a thin, argument-identity-preserving passthrough to `output_schema.build_output` / `build_error_output`. The harness never constructs a `ClaimLicense` on a surface's behalf (§4).
- A markdown report **skeleton**: a title heading, a standard metadata line, and a claim-license-rendered footer. Sampling (§3.4) found these account for at most a handful of lines per surface (4–6 of `render_report`/`render_markdown`'s 40–61 lines in the sampled files) — the bulk of every report function sampled is surface-specific content shape (feature tables, pair listings, tallies) that the harness does not and should not templat.
- `_emit`: writing the JSON/markdown output to file and/or stdout. One canonical signature, replacing the four divergent ones observed in §1.

**The harness never owns, under any mechanism in this document, in any increment:** claim-license CONTENT. No harness-level default for `licenses`, `does_not_license`, or `comparison_set`. Every surface keeps its own license text verbatim, in its own module, and constructs its own `ClaimLicense(...)` (or `_claim_license*`-named function). This is not merely a style preference; §4 explains why it is a load-bearing compatibility requirement for the guard, not just an ethical one.

## 3. Sizing method and result

### 3.1 Why a name-pattern count overstates extraction

The original audit classified LOC as "boilerplate" by function name (`main`, `build_arg_parser`, `compose_envelope`/`render_report`, `_emit`/`_error_envelope`). Applying that same classification to `fallacy_scan.py` (433 LOC) captures 243 lines — 56% of the file — because it counts every line inside those six functions, including the parts of `compose_envelope` that build per-judge-kind caveats (posture-critical prose, not scaffolding) and the parts of `main` that implement the surface's own prompt-fingerprint drift gate (`_effective_judge_fingerprint`, entirely domain logic). Scaled across the codebase this produces something close to the ~31k / 15.8% figure. It is real code, but "matches a boilerplate-shaped function name" is not the same claim as "is mechanically extractable into a shared harness."

### 3.2 Method

Four real top-level surfaces were read in full during drafting and every line classified into exactly one of: (a) mechanically replaceable by a fixed, enumerable harness call with no change to any argument value, (b) domain or posture logic that must stay in the surface, or (c) claim-license content, which stays in the surface by design regardless of mechanism. Samples were chosen to span the file-size distribution (median top-level script size: 666 LOC; p25: 414; p75: 976) rather than cherry-picked. Adversarial review of this draft then supplied two more samples specifically chosen to test whether the four-file sample was representative — both are read below alongside the original four:

| File | LOC | Chosen because |
|---|---:|---|
| `fallacy_scan.py` | 433 | named explicitly in this spec's brief as the prior reviewer's "59% overstates" example |
| `agd_move_scan.py` | 395 | near-clone of `fallacy_scan.py`'s family shape — a second, independent read of the same pattern |
| `voice_profile.py` | 433 | named explicitly in this spec's brief as "80% boilerplate" |
| `position_pair_register.py` | 666 | at the file-size median; carries a runtime posture gate (`_assert_no_banned_keys`) called from inside `compose_envelope`, so it stress-tests whether "envelope composition" can be genericized at all |
| `idiolect_detector.py` | 1,321 | supplied by adversarial review as a counterexample to the four-file sample; not read line-by-line here, but its ≈0.8% figure is structurally corroborated below |
| `kicker_density.py` | 603 | supplied by adversarial review as a counterexample, and the source of the `_SCRIPTS_DIR` bootstrap-naming discovery in §1; its ≈2% figure is structurally corroborated below |

### 3.3 What each sample showed

- **`fallacy_scan.py`**: ≈48 of 433 lines (11.1%) extractable — the `sys.path` bootstrap, the `--json`/`--out`/`--out-md` flags and parser skeleton, `_emit`, `_error_envelope`, and the read-target/`OSError` guard in `main`. Everything else — the judge-kind caveat text in `compose_envelope`, all judge-specific `build_arg_parser` flags, `_effective_judge_fingerprint`'s manifest-vs-current-fingerprint precedence logic, `render_markdown`'s tally/flag-list rendering — is domain logic that does not generalize.
- **`agd_move_scan.py`**: ≈43 of 395 lines (10.9%) — same shape, independently confirming the family pattern rather than one file's idiosyncrasy.
- **`voice_profile.py`**: ≈23 of 433 lines (5.3%) — markedly *lower* than the file explicitly flagged as "80% boilerplate." This file inlines its argparse construction directly in `main()` (no separate `build_arg_parser`) and has no `_emit` at all — it hand-writes three separate privacy-guard-then-write blocks (`--json-out`, `--out`, stdout), a pattern that recurs in `pov_voice_profile.py` and `voice_drift_tracker.py` but not the other ~185 top-level scripts, so it is a *family*-specific duplication, not a harness-scope duplication. The "80%" figure most plausibly measures how much of the file is *not* the stylometric math (which lives in `stylometry_core.build_profile`, imported) rather than how much is harness-extractable; those are different claims, and only the second one is in scope here.
- **`position_pair_register.py`**: ≈51 of 666 lines (7.7%). This file is the clearest illustration of why "envelope composition" cannot be handed wholesale to a harness: `compose_envelope` calls `_assert_no_banned_keys(envelope)` — a runtime gate, unique to this surface, that raises before a relation/verdict key can escape in the envelope — immediately before returning. A harness that owned the return statement outright would either drop that call silently (a posture regression) or need a per-surface post-hook parameter, at which point "the harness owns envelope composition" has quietly become "the harness owns an outer wrapper around a surface-authored function," which is a much smaller claim.
- **`idiolect_detector.py`** (≈0.8%, reviewer-supplied) and **`kicker_density.py`** (≈2%, reviewer-supplied): not independently re-derived line-by-line here, but structurally corroborated — direct inspection confirms neither file defines a separate `build_arg_parser`, `_emit`, `_error_envelope`, or `compose_envelope`; both inline argparse construction directly in `main()`, exactly matching `voice_profile.py`'s low-scoring shape. A near-zero result is therefore the expected outcome of this method, not a surprise: these files simply never had the harness-shaped functions to begin with. `kicker_density.py` additionally uses `_SCRIPTS_DIR` rather than `SCRIPT_DIR` for its bootstrap — the discovery that corrected §1's R1-coverage count from 75 to 85.

**Six samples, not four. Mean 6.3%, range 0.8%–11.1%.** The range is the finding, not the mean: the two families with a separate `build_arg_parser`/`_emit` pair (the judge-scan family) cluster at 10.9%–11.1%; every file sampled that inlines its CLI construction directly into `main()` — `voice_profile.py`, `idiolect_detector.py`, `kicker_density.py`, three of six samples — scores 0.8%–5.3%. A single mean across a demonstrably bimodal-shaped population misrepresents both halves.

### 3.4 What this does and does not license as a population estimate

Applying 0.8%–11.1% to the guard-protected set's 93,039 LOC spans 744–10,327 LOC — too wide to be a useful ceiling on its own, and reporting it without the shape behind it would be exactly the kind of number-laundering this section exists to avoid. The honest statement, stated as this spec's actual sizing claim, is: **roughly 3,000–10,000 LOC, heavily concentrated in a minority of harness-shaped families (a separate `build_arg_parser` plus a matching `_emit`/`_error_envelope` pair — by §1's count, at most the 50 `build_arg_parser`-bearing protected modules, and likely a smaller number once family-exact `_emit` shapes are the binding constraint per §1's four-signature finding), with a long tail of files near zero that inline their CLI construction and have no separate scaffolding functions to extract at all.** This is **not** offered as "consistent with" the ~5–8k LOC figure this spec was originally briefed against, nor with the ~31k audit figure — a six-file, non-random sample validating either prior number would be sample bias wearing a population-estimate's clothing. It explains, mechanically, why the ~31k figure is high (roughly 4.8k of it, §1, is claim-license content no mechanism here may touch, and every boilerplate-*named* function sampled turned out to be dominated by domain or posture logic), and it is a range with a documented shape, not a point estimate. **This is a population range, not Increment 1's yield** — §8 shows the rule-registry-limited Increment 1 scope realistically extracts a few hundred LOC, because most of the 110 protected modules do not share an *exact* structural match with any single proposed rule.

## 4. The compatibility requirement that shapes everything else

The harness must never construct a `ClaimLicense` object, never supply a default `licenses`/`does_not_license` string, and never wrap the `ClaimLicense(...)` call site inside its own code. This is stated as scope in §2; here is why it is load-bearing rather than stylistic.

`check_claim_license_guard.build_protected_set()` seeds its protected set by a naming convention: any module whose AST defines a name matching `_claim_license*` or directly calls `ClaimLicense(...)`. If the harness *itself* constructed `ClaimLicense` objects on behalf of callers (e.g., a `harness.compose(licenses=..., does_not_license=..., ...)` helper that built the `ClaimLicense` internally), then every future surface's actual claim-license content would live inside a call to *harness* code, not inside the surface's own module — and the guard's naming-convention seed detection, which only scans plugin-runtime modules for their *own* `_claim_license*`/`ClaimLicense(...)` sites, would need to either (a) treat the harness module itself as one giant permanently-protected seed carrying every surface's content undifferentiated, which defeats per-surface change tracking entirely, or (b) be taught to trace content back through an indirection layer, which is exactly the kind of interprocedural dataflow analysis the guard's own docstring disclaims ("no claim of general semantic or interprocedural dataflow analysis... fails closed" on anything it cannot resolve to a unique source module).

The design adopted instead avoids the problem rather than solving it: **every surface — adopted or not, forward or legacy — keeps a literal `ClaimLicense(...)` call or `_claim_license*`-named function in its own module**, and passes the *already-constructed* `ClaimLicense` object into `harness.compose(...)` as a value. The guard's existing, unmodified seed-detection logic therefore continues to correctly seed every future surface exactly as it does today, with zero changes to that logic. This is also why the guard amendment in §6 never needs to reason about the harness module at all — the harness is not itself a supplier of license content by construction, so it cannot enter the protected closure and does not need its own carve-out.

## 5. The forward-only ratchet

A new checker — proposed name `check_new_surface_scaffolding`, modeled directly on the "new edges are errors, existing exceptional edges may only disappear" ratchet convention `specs/svp-packaging-conversion.md` describes for its own (not yet built) layering checker — computes the set of `.py` paths present in the candidate tree under `plugins/setec-voiceprint/scripts/` (top-level only) that are **absent from the merge-base tree** (`git diff --diff-filter=A` against `origin/main`, the same merge-base resolution `check_claim_license_guard.merge_base()` already performs). For each such new file that defines its own `TASK_SURFACE`:

- It must import the harness's `_emit`/`error_envelope`/`add_common_output_args` equivalents rather than defining its own module-level `def _emit(`, `def _error_envelope(`, or duplicating the three common flags inside its own `build_arg_parser`.
- A narrow, named exemption file (same shape as packaging's `packaging_migration_exemptions.yaml`: path, reason, owner, introduced SHA) is the only escape hatch, reviewed the same way a layering exemption is reviewed.

Because this checker only ever inspects paths that do not exist at the merge base, it **cannot** touch, re-derive, or interact with `build_protected_set()`'s closure computation in any way — a new file cannot retroactively be "already protected," and the ratchet's pass/fail has no bearing on the Deficit Lock. This is the cleanest possible relationship between two gates: total independence.

**Non-vacuity:** a fixture new top-level file that defines `TASK_SURFACE` and hand-rolls its own `_emit`/`build_arg_parser` must fail the ratchet; the same file rewritten to import the harness equivalents must pass. Both are cheap, deterministic, and require no git history beyond the merge-base diff already being computed.

## 6. The guard amendment

### 6.1 What the current guard proves, and what adoption needs it to keep proving

`compare_protected_modules` today does exactly one thing per protected module: `ast.dump(base, include_attributes=False) == ast.dump(candidate, include_attributes=False)`, whole-module, no exceptions, sourced from `git show <merge-base-sha>:<path>` on one side and the working tree on the other. `load_move_map` additionally requires `moves` and `path_rewrites` to be empty, unconditionally, at P1. Adoption needs *some* declared, bounded class of module deltas to stop being an automatic failure — while preserving, exactly, the property that a real content change is still caught with certainty, not probability.

### 6.2 Four shapes considered and rejected

1. **Owner sign-off in the PR body or a reviewed-artifact file that re-baselines a module's protected AST.** Rejected outright — a re-baseline artifact is the same failure mode wearing a JSON extension: once a human (or a script trusting a human-authored file) can declare "this AST is now the new baseline," the guard's promise degrades from "provably unchanged since the merge base" to "unchanged since whenever someone last re-approved it," which is a materially weaker property with no mechanical way to tell the difference from the outside.
2. **A committed `old_symbol`/`new_symbol` (or `old_text`/`new_text`) substitution row, applied to the dumped AST text before comparison.** This is not a hypothetical risk — it is exactly the mechanism `test_moves_substring_laundering_is_blocked` (§1.1) documents as having been built, found exploitable, and removed from this exact file. A row that can rewrite text can rewrite text on both the base side and the candidate side identically, cancelling a real content delta; that is true regardless of how the row is named, scoped to "only scaffolding," or restricted in length. No shape in this family is proposed anywhere in this document.
3. **Scope comparison to only `_claim_license_relevant_subtrees()`'s output, ignoring the rest of the module.** Rejected because it is unsound, not merely weaker — see §6.4 for the full argument and the corrected exclusion zone.
4. **A code-defined rule registry loaded from the candidate's own working tree.** This was this document's own first-drafted design, and adversarial review constructed a complete, working attack against it (§6.11 walks it in full): CI executes the guard, and therefore the registry it imports, from the PR's checked-out source — so a rule's matcher and replacement are Python in the PR diff, which is author-supplied input to the gate in exactly the sense item 2 already rejects. A malicious or careless PR can add a brand-new rule whose fixed "replacement" is itself a softened license sentence, matched against some unprotected-looking constant, and register that rule against itself in the same diff — the registry never has to be "the same PR that also edits the license," because the registry entry and its use can co-arrive. §6.2's own reasoning for item 2 — "a row that can rewrite text can rewrite text... regardless of how the row is named" — applies to this shape verbatim; the only thing that changed was the file extension. **Both parts of the fix are load-bearing and are described together in §6.3: sourcing the registry from the merge base (so a same-PR rule has zero effect), and mechanically forbidding string content in any rule's replacement (so even a rule that *does* land first, reviewed, on `main` cannot carry prose).**

### 6.3 The mechanism

**A closed, code-defined rule registry, sourced from the merge base, never from the candidate's working tree, and structurally forbidden from carrying string content.** Two invariants are prerequisite to describing any individual rule, because §6.2 item 4 showed that without them the registry degrades to item 2 regardless of how the rules themselves are shaped:

- **Merge-base sourcing.** The registry lives in a sibling module, not `tools/check_claim_license_guard.py` itself (keeping that file's own diff a signal — see §6.9). At verification time, the guard never `import`s the candidate's copy of the registry; it fetches the registry module's source via `git show <merge-base-sha>:<registry-path>` (the exact mechanism `show_file_at` already uses for protected-module content) and evaluates *that* text to obtain rule definitions. A rule referenced by a registration row that does not exist in the merge-base copy of the registry — because it is brand new in this PR, or was renamed, or its definition changed — is an unresolvable rule ID: `GuardError`, not "use whatever's in the working tree." **Consequence, stated as the operative constraint:** a rule can only ever be used in a PR whose merge base already contains it, which means adding or changing a rule requires its own, prior, merged PR that registers zero adoptions — the review of "is this rule's shape safe" and the review of "does this specific module's adoption apply it correctly" are structurally two different PRs, reviewed at two different times, and the second can never smuggle changes into the first.
- **No string content, mechanically enforced.** A rule's `replacement` AST may contain no `ast.Constant` node whose value is `str` or `bytes`, and no `Name`/`Attribute` reference outside a small, explicitly enumerated harness-symbol allowlist (the harness's own public API — `emit`, `error_envelope`, `add_common_output_args`, `build_parser`, `compose`, and the `surface_harness` module name itself). Scaffolding never needs prose: R1's replacement is a bare `import surface_harness` statement; R3–R7's replacements name only harness functions. This check runs twice — as ordinary CI lint on every PR's candidate copy of the registry (§6.9), so a bad rule is caught before it can ever reach a merge base — and, as defense in depth, again by the guard itself against the merge-base copy immediately before use, failing closed (`GuardError`) if it somehow finds a violation there, which should be impossible given the first check but is not assumed to be.

With both invariants in place, here are the rules themselves. Each entry specifies a **matcher** (an exact AST shape, never fuzzy or partial — R1's now captures the bootstrap variable's bound name as a wildcard across statements, per §1's `SCRIPT_DIR`/`_SCRIPTS_DIR`/`ROOT` finding, requiring only that the *same* name is used consistently across the pattern's two statements, never comparing or inserting that name's string value anywhere), an **expected cardinality** (a mismatch is a hard failure, never a partial success), and a **replacement** (fixed, rule-authored, containing no string content per the invariant above). Every rule falls into exactly one of two operation kinds, and the same self-check enforces that no future entry falls outside them:

- **Deletion-with-fixed-insertion**: remove a matched statement or whole function body; insert a fixed, rule-authored replacement. Used for the `sys.path` bootstrap (rule **R1**, now matching 85 of 110 protected modules per §1's corrected count), and for whole hand-rolled `_emit`/`_error_envelope` function bodies matched by name and the exact signature shape found in §1 (rules **R3**/**R4**, scoped to the 6-module judge-scan family in Increment 1). **This operation kind is permanently restricted to two named target shapes and no others:** the sys.path bootstrap pattern, or a whole function definition named exactly `_emit` or `_error_envelope`. `compose_envelope`, `render_report`/`render_markdown`, `build_arg_parser`'s body as a whole, and any `_claim_license*`-named entity are **permanently ineligible** for this operation kind — stated here as a standing prohibition, not merely as an emergent property of the disjointness check in §6.4, so that a future "replace `compose_envelope`'s body wholesale" rule proposal is refused by name, on sight, in review, rather than relying on a downstream check to catch it. (§6.4 explains why, even without this standing prohibition, such a rule would fail the disjointness check on every file sampled — but a standing name-level prohibition is cheaper to review and does not depend on every future protected module happening to make the same shape unsafe.)
- **Callee rename with mandatory argument-value identity**: rewrite `build_output(kw1=v1, kw2=v2, ...)` to `harness.compose(kw1=v1, kw2=v2, ...)` (rule **R5**) or `build_error_output(...)` to `harness.error_envelope(...)` (rule **R6**) or `argparse.ArgumentParser(description=..., prog=...)` to `harness.build_parser(description=..., prog=...)` (rule **R7**) **if and only if** every keyword name and every argument value sub-expression, compared as AST (`ast.dump`, not text), is identical between base and candidate. No rule renames a callee whose arguments differ in any way — a rule whose precondition fails simply does not apply, and an adoption that requires it fails closed.

R2 (argparse common-flag extraction) remains deferred out of Increment 1 for the reason already stated: getting its `"--json"`/`"--out"`/`"--out-md"`-only allowlist verifiably right, and permanently closed to ever including `"--licenses"`/`"--does-not-license"`, is per-family verification work Increment 1 does not attempt (§8).

**Verification, per declared adoption, in order:**

1. A committed file, `harness_adoption_registrations.json`, schema-mirrored on `packaging_move_map.json`: `{"schema": 1, "adoptions": [{"path": "...", "rule_ids": ["R1", "R5", ...]}]}`. This file is PR-writable, and that remains fine under the corrected design for the same reason as before — it selects which already-merged, already-reviewed rules to *attempt*, and authorizes nothing by itself.
2. **Staleness shortcut, checked first (§6.5).** Compare candidate to base with plain whole-module `ast.dump` equality before consulting any rule. If already equal, pass immediately; a registration naming rules for an already-identical module is stale, not wrong, and is reported as an advisory warning, never a failure.
3. If not already equal: resolve every declared `rule_id` against the merge-base-sourced registry (§6.3's first invariant); verify no two declared rules' matched regions overlap in the base AST (§6.6); apply each in the fixed canonical order (§6.6) **forward from the base AST** (never by reverse-engineering the candidate) to produce an expected-candidate AST; require `ast.dump(expected_candidate) == ast.dump(actual_candidate)` exactly.
4. **Independently, regardless of which rules are declared:** compute the exclusion zone via the intra-module dataflow closure (§6.4, replacing the earlier, now-corrected `*LICENSE*`-name-scan design) on both base and candidate; require the ordered sequence of zone spans to match, each pairwise `ast.dump`-identical (§6.6's definition of "positionally identical"); require no rule's matched region overlaps any zone span in either tree.
5. Any ambiguity anywhere in steps 3–4 — an unresolvable rule ID, a cardinality mismatch, an overlapping match, a reconstruction that does not equal the candidate exactly, a zone-span sequence that differs — is a hard failure (`GuardError`, exit 2), matching the existing guard's fail-closed posture.
6. A protected module not listed in `harness_adoption_registrations.json` is checked exactly as today: whole-module identity against the merge base, zero tolerance, zero rule application.

### 6.4 The exclusion zone: an intra-module dataflow closure, not a name scan

§6.2 item 3 and an earlier revision of this document scoped the exclusion zone to `_claim_license_relevant_subtrees()`'s output plus every module-level assignment whose target name contains "LICENSE." **That claim was wrong, and adversarial review found live counterexamples on `origin/main` disproving it directly**, not hypothetically:

- `fast_detect_curvature.py`'s `STUDENT_T_CAVEAT` (defined ~line 130) is appended into `does_not_license_text` via an f-string inside `compose_envelope` (~line 478) — reachable from the `ClaimLicense(...)` call's keyword value, containing genuine refusal-scope prose ("NO p-value is emitted... a t-survival of it would be an UNSUPPORTED transform"), and named with no substring match against "LICENSE."
- `narrative_decision_audit.py`'s `BRIDGE_CONTROL_EXTENSION_SENTENCE` (defined ~line 91, appended into `does_not_license_text` ~lines 456–458) literally contains the sentence "Its output is licensed only as spec-78 Arm A bridge-control input" — license-governing content under a name containing neither "LICENSE" nor `_claim_license`.
- `fallacy_scan.py`'s own `compose_envelope` (~lines 167–196, read in full in §3) builds a local `caveats` list via `caveats.append(...)` calls conditioned on `judge_kind`, then passes `caveats` into `ClaimLicense(additional_caveats=caveats)`. A name-scan finds neither the list's initial binding nor any of its `.append()` call sites, because none of them is a module-level assignment and none is named anything containing "LICENSE."

All three examples share the same shape a name scan cannot see: content reaches the `ClaimLicense(...)` call through a **local variable mutated by method call**, or through a **module constant named for what it is, not for the fact that it contains license text**. The corrected zone is therefore computed by closure, not by name pattern, directly mirroring the philosophy `build_protected_set()` already applies *across* modules (supplier closure) but applied *within* one module, which no part of this spec had done before this review:

1. **Seed.** Every `ast.Name` load inside a `_claim_license_relevant_subtrees()` subtree (a `ClaimLicense(...)` call's arguments, or a `_claim_license*`-named function body).
2. **Closure.** For each name in the frontier, find every binding site for that name anywhere in the module — a module-level or function-local assignment/annotated-assignment target, a function parameter default, *and* every `Call` node shaped `Attribute(value=Name(id=<tracked name>), attr in {"append", "extend", "insert", "update"})` (the list/dict-mutation shape `fallacy_scan.py`'s `caveats` and `position_pair_register.py`'s `warnings` both use). Add each binding statement's value expression (or, for a mutation call, its argument list) to the zone; scan each newly added span for further `Name` loads and add them to the frontier.
3. **Memoize and repeat** until the frontier is empty — the same queue/processed-set shape `build_protected_set`'s cross-module closure already implements, scoped to one module's own statements instead of following imports.
4. The zone is the union of every span visited in steps 1–3, across the whole module, in every function and at module level.

Applied to the three counterexamples: `STUDENT_T_CAVEAT` and `BRIDGE_CONTROL_EXTENSION_SENTENCE` are pulled in at step 2 as soon as the closure reaches the f-string/concatenation expression that references them as a `Name` load inside the `does_not_license_text`-computing statement, which is itself pulled in because `does_not_license_text` is a `Name` loaded inside the `ClaimLicense(...)` call. `fallacy_scan.py`'s `caveats` list is pulled in the same way, and its `.append()` call sites are found by the mutation-call rule in step 2 specifically — the reason that rule exists rather than a plain assignment-only closure.

This closure is still not offered as a *proof* of completeness against every conceivable indirection (a value smuggled through a mutable default argument evaluated once at import time, for instance, is a known further edge this spec does not claim to close) — it is offered as the **legible, review-friendly layer**. §6.3 step 3's whole-module forward-reconstruction-and-equality remains the actual backstop: it does not depend on the zone being complete, because it requires the *entire* module, not just the flagged zone, to reconstruct exactly from base plus the declared rules. The zone's job is a readable failure message and a second, independent check that does not share the reconstruction logic's failure modes — not the primary guarantee.

### 6.5 Registration lifecycle: a stale row must never brick unrelated PRs

§6.3 step 2 (staleness-checked-first) exists to close a specific, concrete failure mode adversarial review constructed: once a module is legitimately adopted and the adoption PR merges, `main` contains both the post-adoption module *and* its now-stale registration row. Every subsequent, entirely unrelated PR's merge base then already contains the adopted shape — R1's bootstrap pattern, for instance, no longer exists in that module to match. Without the staleness shortcut, every one of those later PRs would hit a cardinality mismatch on a module they never touched, and fail. With the shortcut: plain whole-module identity between candidate and base is checked *before* any rule is consulted, for every registered row, on every run; if the module already matches its own merge base, the row is irrelevant to this run — logged as a stale-registration advisory (naming the path, so cleanup is easy to find), never a build failure. Only a row whose module differs from its merge base ever reaches rule verification, which means the rule-application code path is exercised exclusively on PRs that have something real to explain — either a legitimate further adoption step or an attack attempt — which is also why this ordering, per adversarial review's own observation, buys non-vacuity for the fixture suite for free: the reconstruction logic can never coast on an "already trivially equal" shortcut the way an unordered check might.

A registration row naming a path that is not a currently-protected module at all remains a hard `GuardError` at load time (a malformed registration is not the same condition as "stale" — stale means the target is still protected and still merge-base-identical; malformed means the row doesn't describe a real protected module at all).

### 6.6 Rule application order, overlap, and what "positionally identical" means

Two gaps, both closed here: **order** and **overlap** were previously undefined, and the exclusion zone's "positionally identical" requirement was undefined in a way that is self-defeating on its natural reading (R1 deletes statements, so every AST node after the deletion point shifts to a different absolute line/column in the candidate — comparing by absolute position would make R1 incompatible with the zone check by construction).

- **Order.** Declared rules apply in one canonical order — ascending by rule ID string (`R1` before `R3` before `R4` before `R5` before `R6` before `R7`) — never registration-row order, so verification is deterministic regardless of how a registration file lists its `rule_ids`.
- **Overlap.** Before any rule is applied, the guard locates every declared rule's matched region in the base AST and requires all matched regions to be pairwise disjoint. Two declared rules whose matches overlap is a hard failure (`GuardError`) — an ambiguous application is refused, never resolved by picking one.
- **"Positionally identical" (§6.3 step 4)** means: walk the module's statements in source order (module-level statements, and within each function, that function's body statements, in the same traversal both trees use), producing the ordered sequence of exclusion-zone spans encountered. Base and candidate pass only if these sequences have the same length and every pair at the same ordinal position is `ast.dump`-identical. This is defined over relative order and per-span content, never absolute line/column, so it is compatible with R1 (or any other deletion rule) shifting everything after it.

### 6.7 Why this cannot repeat the §6.2 failure, restated for the corrected mechanism

Every rule is a *deletion-of-a-fixed-pattern-with-fixed-insertion* (permanently restricted to two named target shapes, §6.3) or a *rename requiring full argument-value identity*. Neither operation kind can take a string value found in the diff and place it anywhere — the no-string-content invariant makes this a static, checkable property of the rule's own definition, not an argument about what the rule's author "intended." A rule attempting to touch the (now correctly, closure-computed) exclusion zone fails for either of two independent reasons: the zone-disjointness check in §6.3 step 4 refuses any rule whose matched region overlaps a zone span, and — for the deletion operation kind specifically — the target-shape restriction in §6.3 means a rule aimed at `compose_envelope` or any `_claim_license*`-named entity is refused by name before disjointness is even computed. And because the registry itself is merge-base-sourced (§6.3), none of this can be bypassed by introducing a new rule in the same PR that would need it — see §6.11 for the full attack walkthrough.

### 6.8 The weakest point, and its mitigation

This amendment asks the guard to do more than compare two strings for equality: it applies rule transforms to an AST, computes an intra-module dataflow closure, and enforces an ordering/overlap/staleness protocol around all of it. That is substantially more code executing inside a gate whose entire value proposition, to date, has been its simplicity — `ast.dump(a) == ast.dump(b)` is close to unarguable; this pipeline is not, and this revision made the pipeline larger than the one adversarial review already found one hole in. A subtle bug anywhere in it (an off-by-one in a matcher, an incorrect assumption in the closure's traversal order, a cardinality miscount, a staleness check that fires too eagerly) could, in the worst case, manufacture a false equality where none exists. The mitigation is procedural, not a claim that the risk is eliminated: the amendment itself, as a merge-blocking condition, ships the golden-fixture suite in §6.10, including the per-operation-kind adversarial fixtures adversarial review specifically requested, plus a requirement that the transform-and-closure engine's own test suite be run under mutation testing in CI as an acceptance gate (§10) — a fixture suite that passes against a *mutated* copy of the engine (one that, say, silently widens a matcher's cardinality tolerance) is evidence the suite itself is too weak, and CI treats that as a build-blocking finding, not a passing signal. This raises confidence; it does not convert "more code, twice reviewed" into "no more risk than before," and this document does not claim otherwise.

### 6.9 Rule registry governance

The registry lives in a sibling module to `tools/check_claim_license_guard.py`, not inside it — keeping the guard's own file diff-quiet is deliberate: a diff to `check_claim_license_guard.py` itself should stay a rare, high-attention event, and routing all rule changes through a sibling module preserves that property (an earlier draft, before this review, made guard-file edits routine, which erodes exactly this signal). Adding or modifying a rule is an ordinary code change to that sibling module, reviewed at the same bar as this spec, never a data-file edit — and, per §6.3's merge-base-sourcing invariant, has zero effect on any PR's verification until it has landed on `main` in its own PR that registers no adoptions. The no-string-content and operation-kind self-checks (§6.3) run twice: as CI lint against every PR's candidate copy of the registry (blocking a bad rule before it can ever merge), and again by the guard against the merge-base-sourced copy immediately before use (failing closed on a violation that should be structurally impossible given the first check, per the guard's existing posture toward other "impossible" conditions like a missing merge base).

### 6.10 Non-vacuity tests (the demanded proof that the gate can fail)

Modeled on `test_check_claim_license_guard.py`'s `fake_repo` fixture idiom (§1.1), expanded per adversarial review to cover the corrected mechanism's new failure modes and to include per-operation-kind adversarial cases, not only the two attacks this document anticipated on its own:

1. **Legitimate adoption passes.** A `fake_repo` module built from `fallacy_scan.py`'s real `sys.path` bootstrap plus `_emit` shape, with the declared rules already present in the (fixture's simulated) merge-base-sourced registry, registered with `rule_ids: ["R1", "R3"]`, transformed exactly as R1/R3 specify → `clg.run()` passes.
2. **The planted-softening fixture.** The same base module, but the candidate's `ClaimLicense(...)` call has one word changed in `does_not_license`, while the rest of the file is transformed exactly per R1/R3 and registered accordingly → `clg.run()` must fail via the whole-module reconstruction mismatch (§6.3 step 3) and, separately, via the exclusion-zone identity check (§6.3 step 4) — both fixtures required, not just one.
3. **The Finding-1 same-PR rule attack (§6.11), reproduced as a fixture.** A candidate PR that both (a) adds a new rule to the registry sibling module and (b) registers an adoption using that rule in the same diff → `GuardError` at rule-resolution time (§6.3: the rule does not exist in the merge-base-sourced registry). This is the specific attack adversarial review constructed; it must be a named, permanent regression test, not merely covered in prose.
4. **A rule whose replacement carries a string `Constant`, submitted to the registry's own self-check.** Independent of any adoption — a unit test against the self-check function itself, asserting it refuses such a rule definition outright.
5. **Cardinality mismatch fails closed.** A registration declares `R1` for a module whose bootstrap block does not exactly match the pattern under any of the three known names → `GuardError`, not a silent skip.
6. **Overlapping matched regions fail closed.** Two declared rules whose matchers both claim an intersecting AST region → `GuardError`, per §6.6.
7. **Stale registration passes with a warning, not a failure.** A row registers `rule_ids` for a module whose candidate already equals its base exactly (simulating "already adopted in a prior merge") → `clg.run()` passes, and the report notes the row as stale.
8. **A row for a module unrelated to any change in the PR does not brick that PR.** The exact §6.5 scenario: an untouched, previously-adopted module with a leftover row, in a PR that changes something else entirely → passes.
9. **Exclusion-zone closure catches the three counterexamples.** Three fixtures, one modeled on each of `fast_detect_curvature.py`'s `STUDENT_T_CAVEAT`, `narrative_decision_audit.py`'s `BRIDGE_CONTROL_EXTENSION_SENTENCE`, and `fallacy_scan.py`'s `caveats.append(...)` pattern — a one-word edit to the indirected content, with no rule declared to explain it, must fail via the closure-based zone check.
10. **Ratchet non-vacuity** (§5): a new top-level file defining `TASK_SURFACE` with a hand-rolled `_emit` fails `check_new_surface_scaffolding`; the same file importing the harness's `emit` passes.

Every guard-run test above also gains a companion assertion on the report's new `adoptions_applied` field (§10): for a passing run, it lists exactly which rules fired at which matched spans, so a reviewer can see what happened without re-deriving it — the same visible-application discipline the concurrently-developed hermetic-mode amendment already applies to its own exemptions (§9.2).

### 6.11 The Finding-1 attack, walked against the corrected mechanism

Adversarial review's constructed attack against the first draft: add a rule `R_evil` whose matcher targets `STUDENT_T_CAVEAT`'s assignment in `fast_detect_curvature.py` (no `LICENSE` in the name, outside the old name-scan zone) with a fixed, rule-authored, softened replacement string; register the module against `R_evil`; the reconstruction step would pass (base + `R_evil` == candidate, by construction); the old exclusion-zone check would pass (the `ClaimLicense(...)` call site itself is untouched); the original fixtures would pass (they targeted words inside the call's keyword value, not a rule's own baked-in replacement text). Walking it against the mechanism above:

- `R_evil` is defined in the same PR that would use it. Per §6.3's merge-base-sourcing invariant, the guard resolves `rule_ids` against the merge-base copy of the registry, which does not contain `R_evil` — **unresolvable rule ID, `GuardError`, before any AST comparison happens at all.**
- Suppose instead the attacker first lands `R_evil` in its own, separate, reviewed PR, hoping ordinary review misses that its replacement is a softened string. The no-string-content self-check (§6.3, run as CI lint on that PR) statically rejects any rule whose replacement contains an `ast.Constant` string — **`R_evil` cannot pass CI as a candidate rule, let alone merge.**
- Suppose the self-check itself were somehow bypassed and `R_evil` did land. `STUDENT_T_CAVEAT` is now reachable by the corrected exclusion zone specifically because it is the counterexample §6.4's closure was built to catch — reached via `does_not_license_text = f"{does_not_license_text} {STUDENT_T_CAVEAT}"`, a `Name` load inside the statement computing the `does_not_license` keyword value. `R_evil`'s matched region (the `STUDENT_T_CAVEAT` assignment) would overlap a zone span — **refused by §6.3 step 4's disjointness requirement**, independent of the reconstruction step.
- And `R_evil`'s operation kind is deletion-with-fixed-insertion targeting a plain module-level constant, which is not one of the two permanently eligible target shapes (§6.3) — **refused by name in review, before either of the above checks is even reached.**

Four independent layers now stand between this attack and a green gate, where the first draft had zero. The attack does not succeed against the mechanism in this section.

## 7. Optional adoption outside the protected closure

The 4 modules identified in §1 (`setec_run_set.py`, `restoration_packet.py`, `before_after_restoration.py`, `diveye_signals.py`) may adopt the harness with **zero interaction with the guard mechanism in §6** — they are, by the guard's own oracle, outside the protected closure, so ordinary code review is the only gate. This is worth doing precisely because it is free, not because the yield is large: at the §3.4 sampled range, 4,255 LOC yields on the order of a couple hundred lines, plausibly more given these four were not among the six samples and could as easily land near the judge-scan family's 11% as near the near-zero tail. The remaining 26 of the original 30 candidates should **not** be pushed into this harness at all — they do not call `build_output`/`build_error_output`, and forcing an envelope-shaped harness onto acquisition scripts that were never shaped like a capability surface is scope creep this spec explicitly declines.

- Coordination with the concurrently-developed hermetic-mode guard amendment (§9.2) beyond the single exclusivity constraint stated there — this document does not know that amendment's internal design and does not propose one for it.
- Any change to `output_schema.py`, `claim_license.py`, or the `ClaimLicense` constructor signature itself. This spec designs the *mechanism* that would let a future breaking contract change fan out safely; it does not propose that change.

## 9. Sequencing: in place, not after relocation

Adopt in place on the current flat tree. Three reasons, none of them merely "packaging said so" (packaging's own §3 explicitly disclaims authority over this question — it withdrew the harness from *its* scope, it did not mandate an order for a future harness spec):

1. **Each file is touched once.** If adoption instead waited for packaging's P4 (family-by-family relocation, ~10 PRs by that spec's own phase count), every adopted module would be edited twice — once for the physical move, once for harness adoption — doubling guard interaction and review surface for no benefit.
2. **The rule registry in §6.3 is written against today's import shape** (`from claim_license import ClaimLicense`, the flat `sys.path` bootstrap R1 targets). §9.1 shows this shape survives P2 unchanged, because P2's compatibility launchers preserve the flat import path by contract — but P4's eventual package-relative imports are a real, not merely hypothetical, change to the statements R1/R5/R6/R7 match (a surface module relocated under `setec.surfaces` would plausibly import `from setec.contract import build_output` or similar, a different statement than today's `from output_schema import build_output`). Waiting for P4 would mean a second registry designed and re-reviewed for the post-relocation shape, duplicating §6's review cost for zero additional capability — P2 is not the risk here, P4 is, and reason 1 already covers P4.
3. **Harness value is orthogonal to physical file location.** A shared `_emit`/argparse/envelope helper works identically whether the caller lives at `plugins/setec-voiceprint/scripts/fallacy_scan.py` today or at a P4-relocated path inside `setec.surfaces` later. There is no technical dependency running from harness adoption to relocation in either direction — and adoption may, incidentally, make P4 *easier* later: post-adoption modules converge on a smaller set of `(has_main, has_TASK_SURFACE)`-adjacent shapes, which is exactly what packaging's launcher-template classification (§2 of `specs/svp-packaging-conversion.md`) already keys off.

This does not argue the ~10-PR P4 tail is unnecessary — packaging's stated motivations for it (zero-install layering, `sys.path` hygiene) are independent of this spec's LOC concerns — only that nothing about *this* spec should be gated on it, and that doing this work first does not foreclose or complicate P4 landing afterward.

### 9.1 Invariance under P2, specifically, which is queued now

P2 is the nearer-term concern the P4 argument above did not address: it relocates `output_schema.py`, `claim_license.py`, and `capabilities.py` behind pinned compatibility launchers and, per packaging's own §2 and its explicit statement that "compatibility launchers remain for the life of schema 1.x," the old flat paths (`plugins/setec-voiceprint/scripts/output_schema.py`, `plugins/setec-voiceprint/scripts/claim_license.py`) continue to exist and continue to re-export the relocated implementation. Every rule in §6.3 matches statements *inside surface modules* — `fallacy_scan.py`'s own `sys.path` bootstrap (R1), `fallacy_scan.py`'s own `build_output(...)` call site (R5) — never anything inside `output_schema.py`/`claim_license.py` themselves. Because P2 explicitly preserves `from output_schema import build_output` and `from claim_license import ClaimLicense` as valid, unchanged imports from any surface module's perspective, **P2 landing does not change a single byte of any surface module R1/R3/R4/R5/R6 match against**, and the rules are invariant under it without requiring Increment 1 to race P2 to merge first. This is a demonstration, not merely an assertion of ordering; it holds because packaging's own compatibility-launcher contract is exactly the guarantee that makes it hold.

### 9.2 Coordination with other guard-extension mechanisms

`harness_adoption_registrations.json` is one of, at minimum, two guard-extension mechanisms now in flight against the same protected set — the other being the queued hermetic-mode amendment packaging's own text defers to "its own reviewed PR." Left unaddressed, two independent amendments each adding their own pass condition to the same guard combine as an **OR**: a module could pass because either mechanism's check succeeds, which is a strictly weaker combined gate than either mechanism alone intends, and the attack surface becomes the union of both. This spec's requirement, stated as a coordination constraint on both amendments rather than a redesign of the other one (whose internal design this document does not have visibility into): **a protected module registered under `harness_adoption_registrations.json` for a given merge-base comparison must not, in that same comparison, also be covered by any other mechanism's exemption path, and vice versa.** Each protected module has at most one active guard-extension mechanism per comparison; if both this spec's registration and the hermetic-mode amendment's exemption ever name the same path, that is itself a hard failure requiring the conflict to be resolved by removing one, not a case either mechanism's implementation resolves silently by trying both.

## 10. Acceptance gates (computable predicates)

1. `check_new_surface_scaffolding` (§5) exits non-zero on any new top-level `.py` file (absent from the merge base) that defines `TASK_SURFACE` and hand-rolls `_emit`/`_error_envelope`/the three common flags, with no unlisted-exemption escape.
2. The amended `check_claim_license_guard.py`: for every module in `harness_adoption_registrations.json`, the staleness shortcut (§6.5) runs first; only a module differing from its own merge base proceeds to rule resolution against the **merge-base-sourced** registry (§6.3) — a rule ID absent from that merge-base copy is `GuardError`, not a working-tree fallback — then the ordering/overlap check (§6.6), then the forward-transform-then-whole-module-equality check, then the independent closure-based exclusion-zone identity check (§6.4). Every protected module not listed continues to require exact whole-module identity, unchanged from today.
3. The no-string-content and operation-kind self-checks (§6.3, §6.9) run both as CI lint against every PR's candidate copy of the registry sibling module, and again by the guard against the merge-base-sourced copy immediately before use; either failing is `GuardError`.
4. A rules-only PR (§8 step 0) that both defines and uses a new rule in the same diff is impossible by construction — verified by the §6.11 attack fixture (§6.10 item 3) as a permanent regression test, not merely asserted in prose.
5. All ten §6.10 fixtures and the §5 ratchet fixture pass, including every fixture that asserts the gate **fails** (planted softening ×2, the Finding-1 same-PR attack, a string-content rule, cardinality mismatch, overlapping matches, each exclusion-zone closure counterexample) — a suite with zero failing-case assertions is rejected as non-vacuous-incomplete per this repo's own recurring finding about gates that cannot fail.
6. The transform-and-closure engine's own test suite is run under mutation testing in CI (§6.8); a mutation that survives (a mutated engine the suite fails to catch) is a build-blocking finding for the rules-only PR, not a follow-up.
7. Every passing guard run against a non-empty `harness_adoption_registrations.json` emits an `adoptions_applied` field enumerating, per adopted module, which rules fired and their matched-region spans — and a stale registration (§6.5) is reported as a distinct, named advisory, never silently folded into a bare pass.
8. `packaging_move_map.json` and `harness_adoption_registrations.json` are validated independently; neither schema references or depends on the other. No protected module path appears in both `harness_adoption_registrations.json` and any other guard-extension mechanism's exemption artifact for the same comparison (§9.2).
9. Existing `tools/check_claim_license_guard.py` tests (`test_check_claim_license_guard.py`, currently 496 lines / dozens of cases per §1.1) continue to pass unmodified — the amendment is additive to the module's public surface, not a rewrite of `compare_protected_modules`'s default (unregistered) code path.
10. `python3 tools/spec_anchor_lint.py --spec specs/svp-surface-harness.md --repo .` returns zero HIGH-confidence gating findings.
11. `python3 tools/check_docs_freshness.py` passes (this spec introduces no `capabilities.d` entry, so the CHANGELOG-coverage check is unaffected by construction).

## 11. Out of scope

- Any change to `output_schema.py`, `claim_license.py`, `ClaimLicense`'s constructor, or `capabilities.py` — the L0 contract modules stay exactly as they are.
- Rule R2 and any protected-module family beyond the 6-module judge-scan set — future increments, each its own reviewed rules-only PR per §6.9 and §8's step-0 ordering, never addable in the same PR that registers an adoption using it.
- The 26 acquisition/utility modules outside the protected closure that do not build an envelope (§1, §7) — not a harness target regardless of guard status.
- Packaging's P2–P5 (`specs/svp-packaging-conversion.md`) — untouched, unblocked, and not a prerequisite (§9), and demonstrated invariant under P2 specifically (§9.1).
- The internal design of the concurrently-developed hermetic-mode guard amendment — this spec states only the exclusivity constraint needed to keep the two mechanisms' combined pass condition an AND rather than an OR (§9.2); it does not review or redesign the other amendment.
- A wheel/distribution build, `pip install`, or any packaging-format change — inherited as out of scope from the packaging spec and not reopened here.
- Retroactively re-scoping any already-shipped capability's `ClaimLicense` content "while we're in the file" — adoption under this spec is scaffolding-only by construction (§6.7); a genuine content change to any surface remains its own, separately reviewed PR, exactly as `specs/svp-packaging-conversion.md` already establishes for packaging moves.
